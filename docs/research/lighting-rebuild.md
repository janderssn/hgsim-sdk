# Alpine lighting and cloud rebuild

Checked 2026-11-08 against the actual meadow and staging captures and the retained illustrated alpine concept. This research informs original shader changes; no third-party cloud image or renderer package is bundled.

## Source review

- [Three.js Sky.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/9c4515e66f479864e39b72f29e10260821576b4a/examples/jsm/objects/Sky.js), inspected at commit `9c4515e66f479864e39b72f29e10260821576b4a`. MIT. Its current cloud implementation shares solar and sky radiance, controls cloud coverage independently from density, uses Beer/powder response for cloud mass, and composites the clouds through atmospheric extinction. Relevant lesson: hue should survive the radiance and tone-mapping path; a bright white overlay is insufficient. The local renderer already has a volumetric medium and world-space shadows, so replacing it with this screen-projected noise would lose useful continuity. No source was copied.
- [Takram three-geospatial](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial/tree/b012ad06d858fc035d88aacfd73f092f93c994e4), inspected at commit `b012ad06d858fc035d88aacfd73f092f93c994e4`; [README](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial/blob/b012ad06d858fc035d88aacfd73f092f93c994e4/README.md) and [MIT license](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial/blob/b012ad06d858fc035d88aacfd73f092f93c994e4/LICENSE). It separates precomputed atmospheric scattering and volumetric clouds, and documents WebGL/WebGPU support. Relevant lesson: the sky, terrain extinction, sunlight and environment lighting must agree. Adding its geospatial renderer wholesale is unnecessary for a metre-scale Three r180 range with an existing bounded WebGL2 atmosphere.

## Implemented response

The old key came from behind the normal launch/landing approach. The visible front of the prop kit and trees stayed dark and cyan, even after a warm grade. The afternoon sun now comes from the camera-side right quadrant; the same direction and colour still drive direct lighting, sky radiance, diffuse SH, metal reflections and cloud shadows. Its warm incident light can reveal the material's shape before grading. Diffuse sky and grade reduce cyan contamination while retaining cool open shadows.

Cloud density now takes most of its shape from rounded cellular lobes, with lower-weight lattice noise for modulation. Eleven compact periodic weather envelopes replace broad irregular noise islands, leaving distinct blue-sky corridors. Weaker erosion keeps the billows connected instead of cutting the bank into pink/grey dust; the common lower height profile keeps flat bases beneath domed tops. It uses the existing 3D texture, periodic field, weather masks and shadow integral. This changes no sample counts, target sizes or texture allocations. Cloud illumination uses lower ambient and direct radiance with a bounded density response so peach highlights and slate undersides survive tone mapping instead of approaching white together. Bloom is reduced to retain edges.

The 14 atmosphere and finishing checks pass, including CPU/GPU-style radiance correspondence, deterministic periodic density, cloud-shadow integration, state restoration and quality resource caps. Parent integration captures provide the visual acceptance evidence; these tests alone do not establish a concept match or GPU performance.

Further image review found cotton-like towers and excessive distant forest contrast. Cloud lobes now span twice the world-space width, with a thinner 800m vertical layer and broader weather envelopes. A smooth artistic optical-depth scale begins beyond 700m and reaches full strength at 3km, preserving the immediate flying corridor while collapsing distant forest detail into cooler blue layers. The same scaling is implemented in the CPU transmission reference and shared GLSL; a test checks near preservation, distant contrast reduction, continuity and reciprocal paths.
